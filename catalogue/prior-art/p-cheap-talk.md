## Purpose

Cheap talk has only babbling equilibria under opposed interests; alignment buys partition resolution.

## Content

Crawford & Sobel, "Strategic Information Transmission": under opposed interests, cheap-talk games have only babbling equilibria — a sender with different incentives than the receiver cannot credibly transmit fine-grained information, and the receiver rationally ignores the channel. As interests align, informative equilibria become available, and alignment buys **resolution**: the number of distinguishable message classes the equilibrium supports grows with the degree of alignment.

This is the formal basis for phase 3's (`catalogue/phases.md`) alignment-scalar design: the `w = 0` prediction (babbling, the actor learns to ignore the reporter) and the `w = 1` prediction (fully aligned informant, honest reporting) both come directly from this theory, and the intermediate-`w` region — where partial credibility must be established — is the interesting region precisely because the theory predicts a *graded* resolution rather than a threshold. The reconciled amendments (R30) add the specific prediction that the number of distinguishable message classes should grow with `w`, which is a concrete, falsifiable readout to log once phase 3 runs.
